Why Does Space Look So Dark?

ASTRONOMY

Reading time: 4 minutes

An Apparently Simple Mystery

Look up at the night sky. You can see stars shining against a dark background. But if the universe contains an enormous number of stars, why isn't the entire sky bright? This question is connected to a concept known as Olbers' paradox.

Light Needs to Reach Us

We see objects when light reaches our eyes. Stars produce their own light, while planets and other objects can reflect light from nearby stars. However, light from extremely distant objects takes a very long time to reach Earth. Some light is still travelling towards us and has not arrived yet.

The Universe Is Expanding

The universe has been expanding over time. As space expands, light travelling through it can become stretched to longer wavelengths. This effect is called cosmological redshift. Some light from distant galaxies becomes so stretched that it is no longer visible to the human eye.

Why Is Space Dark, Then?

Space does not have an atmosphere like Earth's. On Earth, sunlight scatters through the atmosphere, making the daytime sky appear blue. In space, there is very little material to scatter sunlight in the same way. As a result, the background appears dark, even when the Sun is shining on nearby objects.

A Universe Still Waiting to Be Understood

The darkness of space is not evidence that the universe is empty. It reminds us that what we can see depends on how light travels and what our instruments can detect. There is still much to learn about the universe, and every question gives us another reason to explore.
